This study successfully developed and evaluated a Continuous Latent Autoencoder (CLAE), a highly efficient 23.8M-parameter speech foundation model trained entirely from scratch for 16 kHz Bengali speech. By leveraging a novel self-supervised learning objective that combines mel-spectrogram reconstruction, Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), and Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization (VISReg), we generated robust frame-level representations at a 12.5 Hz latent sequence rate. Our findings challenge the prevailing paradigm in low-resource speech AI, which predominantly relies on fine-tuning computationally expensive multilingual models. We demonstrated that building a compact, dedicated foundation model from scratch—featuring a 15.29M-parameter frozen feature extractor—is a viable pathway that yields highly competitive, and in specific metrics, superior performance compared to heavyweight state-of-the-art models. While this research establishes a resource-efficient standard for Bengali speech representation, future work will focus on evaluating these representations across downstream tasks such as Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) and Speech Emotion Recognition. Additionally, expanding the pre-training dataset to include diverse regional dialects and exploring advanced quantization techniques will further enhance the model's robustness and enable seamless deployment on edge devices.
