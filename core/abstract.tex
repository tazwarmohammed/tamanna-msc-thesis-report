\section*{Abstract}
The rapid advancement of self-supervised learning (SSL) has revolutionized speech representation, yet low-resource languages like Bengali remain largely dependent on fine-tuning massive, computationally expensive multilingual foundation models. This reliance not only imposes prohibitive resource constraints but also frequently overlooks the unique phonetic nuances of the Bengali language. To address this critical gap, this thesis introduces a Continuous Latent Encoder (CLE)—a highly efficient, 23.8M-parameter speech foundation model developed entirely from scratch for 16 kHz Bengali speech. Trained exclusively on publicly available datasets using a novel self-supervised objective that optimally combines mel-spectrogram reconstruction, Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), and Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization (VISReg), our approach generates robust, frame-level representations at a 12.5 Hz latent sequence rate. Despite its remarkably compact footprint—featuring a 15.29M-parameter frozen feature extractor—the proposed model achieves highly competitive, and in specific evaluation metrics, superior performance compared to state-of-the-art heavyweight speech models. This research breaks new ground by unequivocally demonstrating that training a dedicated, lightweight foundation model from scratch is not only a viable pathway for low-resource speech AI but also yields highly effective representations for downstream analysis. By providing a scalable, resource-efficient architecture, this work establishes a new paradigm for accessible and specialized speech representation learning in Bengali, paving the way for future innovations in low-resource linguistic technologies.
